## Fix 1 — Export rows take only a storage's excess above its Desired Amount

### Where the drain comes from (confirmed on the page)

1. **The Export row hands the station vanilla's "send" policy.** `D.Apply`
   (`40_TrainDistribution.lua:294-322`) maps an Export row to a transient transport policy of
   `"send"` and calls vanilla's `Station:SetDesiredAmount`. Vanilla's send branch
   (`Lua/Buildings/Station.lua:982-987`) sets the station's demand request desired amount to **0**
   and its supply request desired amount to **max**, with the comment "set as if desired is max".
2. **A demand request's desired amount is "room not wanted".** Every storage writes it as
   `max - desired_amount` (`Lua/Buildings/StorageDepot.lua:68`,
   `Lua/Buildings/MultiResourceDepot.lua:221`, `Lua/Buildings/ResourceStockpile.lua:332`). A demand
   desired of 0 means the station wants filling to its physical capacity.
3. **The station's demand is a storage request, branded like a depot's.** `MultiResourceDepot.lua:33-34`
   (the station's base) and `StorageDepot.lua:353-354` (the Food Depot) carry the same
   `rfStorageDepot` flags, and every such request sits at priority 0
   (`CommonLua/TaskRequest.lua:183-189`). A depot-to-station haul is therefore vanilla's own
   depot-to-depot rebalance, decided inside the C matcher behind `Lua/_TaskRequest.lua:74-86`
   (`Request_FindTask`). No Lua decides it; `Request_FindSupply` / `Request_FindDemand` are the
   same C seam (`:56-72`).
4. **Vanilla has no supply-side floor.** The slider's own tooltip
   (`Lua/XDef/ipWasteRockSite.generated.lua:71`): "Drones and Shuttles will attempt to stockpile at
   least <DesiredAmountUI> ... The storages with higher Desired Amount will be filled first ... The
   stored resources will still be used when needed." Engine fact `EF-059` measured the same:
   `desired_amount` withholds nothing from the supply side. A station declared "as if desired is
   max" is the highest-desired storage on the map, so it outranks every depot and pulls them below
   their own floors. That is the owner's screenshot (`StorageFood` Desired 50 at 8.2/180 beside a
   `Food · Export` row at 16.5/120) at mechanism level.
5. **Nothing else hauls.** `30_TrainHubDrones.lua` makes no `FindTask` / `FindSupplyRequest` /
   `FindDemandRequest` call of its own (grep: none), so the vanilla matcher is the only actor.
6. **The row's slider never reaches the drone side today.** Vanilla's send branch ignores the
   amount, and spec §4.8's mode table says Export drones keep the station full; the slider is the
   trains' floor (`train_view`, `40_TrainDistribution.lua:599-612`). No slider behaviour changes.

### The change (narrowest; rung 1, same rung as today)

Stop the station from pulling. Export's drone baseline becomes **demand desired = max, supply
desired = max** instead of today's 0 and max. The station then asks for nothing on its own
initiative and gives nothing away; what reaches it is whatever vanilla pushes into a storage that
wants nothing.

- Where: `D.Apply`, after the existing `pcall(set_desired, st, n)` at
  `40_TrainDistribution.lua:316`, one extra write for `entry.mode == "export"`:
  `st.demand[res]:SetDesiredAmount(st:GetMaxStorageForAnyOneResource())`. That is the request
  writer `Station:SetDesiredAmount` itself calls (`Station.lua:978,984,989`), so the state stays
  vanilla-shaped. The header comment's "Export's drone baseline is full" line changes to say
  "Export's drone baseline pulls nothing".
- Residual without the mod: unchanged class. A player who touches the slider or the accept state
  rewrites both numbers (`Station.lua:1038-1039` store, `:1044-1045` export), and `vanilla_baseline`
  (`40_TrainDistribution.lua:288-292`) still restores the dial at save for hubless rows.
- No persisted name moves. No ban is crossed. Hub-served and hubless rows share `D.Apply`, so one
  change covers both; brief 35's battery carries the hub-served run.
- Stores with no Desired Amount of their own: a producer's output stockpile creates its supply
  request with `desired_amount` (default 0) and no storage brand
  (`ResourceStockpile.lua:328-332`), so `EF-059` says it wins every pairing before any depot and
  goes to any storage with room. **The call:** all of a producer's output is excess, exactly as
  vanilla feeds a depot. Record it in the commit with these lines.
- UI text to revisit in the same commit: the Export tooltips at `45_TrainDistributionUI.lua:41`
  and `:47` say "Local drones fill this station"; the wording depends on the measured outcome
  below.

### The one open engine question, and the two outcomes

No Lua states whether the C matcher pushes a depot's stock above its own Desired Amount into a
storage that has no deficit. The smoke decides it:

| outcome | what the owner sees | status against the ruling |
|---|---|---|
| (a) depots push their excess | the Desired-50 depot settles at 50; the station gains that excess plus producer output | fully met |
| (b) depots push nothing | the depot keeps everything; the station fills from producers and loose piles only | "loses nothing" met; "fills from excess" not met |

If (b) is measured, brief stop 3 applies and the options are:

1. **A wrapper at the FindTask seam** (`EF-060`): when the matcher pairs a storage supply below
   its desired with an Export row's demand, re-query or veto. Cost: Lua on the hottest drone seam
   (25,184 calls per 10 game hours in `EF-060`'s sample), a starvation risk if the veto keeps
   meeting the same best pairing, and behaviour the engine never had. Rung 0.
2. **Accept (b)** and reword the Export tooltip to "producers' output and loose piles fill this
   station; depots keep their Desired Amount". Cost: the "fills from excess" half of the ruling
   waits.
3. Pulling with a lower desired (demand desired = max minus slider) is **not** a candidate: every
   pull drains the source below its floor (item 4 above).

Whatever the matcher does is filed as an engine fact in phase 2 (`smr-bug-library`), with the
sitting's log as its measurement.

## Fix 2 — auto-fill's train enters at the joining station

### The audit's reading: confirmed

  hub arm (`:93-97`) and assigns on the hub. Confirmed.
- `:76-91` is `OnMsg.TransportLinkChanged`: when the vehicle's `current_station` is a hub and it is

  (`20_TrainHub.lua:402-404`, the computed siding of `synthetic_spot_pos(..., "Spawn", idx)`
  `:362-387`). Confirmed. Commit `9b58888` says it covers "extras, auto-fill and player spawns".
- `HubTrain` (`70_TrainBay.lua:6-9`) is a bare class with `persist_baseclass = "Train"`, persisted
  name #15 (`FIX_POLICY.md:179`). No other dev file, test or bug record references it (grep:
  none outside the bay file). It stays; nothing here touches it.

### How a train comes to enter at a station (vanilla)

`TrackBase:AssignTrain(station)` (`Lua/Buildings/Track.lua:428-457`) is the only spawn. It places
the Train **in the station passed**: `PlaceObjectIn("Train", station)`, position and angle from the
station's `Spawn<idx>` spot (`GetSpawnPoint`, `:206-209`, idx = `station:GetConnectionSpot(track)`),
`current_station = station`, `at_spawn_track = true`, then `AddTransportLink` (which sends
`TransportLinkChanged` with action `"add"`, `Lua/Buildings/StationsLink.lua:36-47`),
`AssignToTrack`, `station:AddOccupyingTrain(train)` and `Start()`. The spawn host is simply the
station argument. Vanilla's two callers both pass the station the train belongs to:

- the player's **Assign to this Track** interaction (`Station.lua:861-868`, entered through
  `ToggleCreateRouteMode` `:796-803`, eligibility in `CanInteractWithObject` `:834-856`: connected
  track, reaches another station, route under cap, spawn platform free);
- **train construction** (`ConstructTrainInternal`, `:579-612`): one prefab added, then the single
  connected track whose route is under capacity gets `AssignTrain(self)`; two eligible routes
  means no assignment.

A train born `at_spawn_track` leaves on its own: `LoadTrain` treats the spawn state as
`should_move` and may reverse onto its own track (`Lua/Units/Train.lua:237-258`),
`GetArrivalTrack` uses its own track as the departure (`:208-218`), and the station's
`TrainDepart` clears the flag on the way out (`Station.lua:1209`). So a train assigned at the
joining station behaves exactly as a player-assigned one there, which is the owner's ask.

### The change

`try_fill` assigns at the joining station instead of the hub:

1. **Remember who joined.** `take_snapshot` (`70_TrainBay.lua:133-149`) today keeps only a station
   count per arm. It keeps the route's station handle set instead (`route_key` already builds the
   sorted handle list, `:36-43`), and when a line grows, the owed entry records the joining
   station: `owed[line.key] = { since = GameTime(), station = <joined> }`. If several stations
   join in one rebuild, the joining station is the new one that a connected track links to a
   station already on the route; fallback, the lowest handle. The call is recorded in the commit.
2. **Pick the track at that station.** `station:ForEachConnectedTrack(function(track, dest) ... end)`
   (`Lua/TrainTransport.lua:68-80`, the walker vanilla's own construction path uses) over tracks
   whose `city.train_track_routes[track]` is this line's route, taking the first with
   `track:CanAddVehicle()` and no `station:GetOccupyingTrain(track)`. Then
   `track:AssignTrain(station)`. `CanAddVehicle` is route-wide (`Track.lua:423-426`,
   `GetTrainsOnRoute`), so the cap holds whichever track is used.
3. **The existing window and retry stay** (`:100-101`, two game hours, a tick every ten minutes).
   The hub-arm search (`free_arm`, `:93-97`) and the arm field of the fill log line go; the fill
   line names the line key and the joining station's handle instead.
4. If the joining station is no longer valid or on the route when the tick fires, the owed entry
   is dropped, as an expired window is today.

Spec §4.8 ruling 10's "placing a spawned train on its arm's siding" clause already carries the
owner's 2026-10-02 amendment; the module record for the bay gets the same sentence in phase 2.

### What still needs the hub-side siding placement: keep it, unchanged

The handler at `:76-91` keys on the spawned vehicle's `current_station` being a hub. After the
change, auto-fill never produces that case, but vanilla still can:

- The hub inherits `Station`, and the dev mod hides neither `ToggleCreateRouteMode` nor the
  `assign_train` interaction (grep over `tools/devmods/train_hub/`: no match). A player pressing
  vanilla's assign button on a hub's card reaches `AssignTrain(hub)`. Whether vanilla's panel shows
  that button on the hub is unmeasured; the path exists in code.
- `AssignTrain` reads `Spawn<idx>` through the hub's `GetSpotBeginIndex` override
  (`20_TrainHub.lua:435-443`), which synthesises the spot because the body carries none; brief 15's
  live counterexample (commit `9b58888`'s message) showed that spot-only placement lands wrong, which
  is why the handler exists.
- `HubRestoreParkedTrains` (`20_TrainHub.lua:564-580`) re-places `at_spawn_track` trains at a hub on
  load and is untouched.

So the handler stays as the player-spawn-at-hub correction, and its `[TrainBay] spawn` log line
becomes the smoke's absence witness: an auto-filled train's handle must not appear in one.
