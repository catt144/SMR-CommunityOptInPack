## Fix 1 — Export rows take only a storage's excess above its Desired Amount

### Where the drain comes from (confirmed on the page)

1. **The Export row hands the station vanilla's "send" policy.** `D.Apply`
   (`40_TrainDistribution.lua:294-322`) maps an Export row to a transient transport policy of
   `"send"` and calls vanilla's `Station:SetDesiredAmount`. Vanilla's send branch
   (`Lua/Buildings/Station.lua:982-987`) sets the station's demand request desired amount to **0**
   and its supply request desired amount to **max**, with the comment "set as if desired is max".
2. **A demand request's desired amount is "room not wanted".** Every storage writes it as
   `max - desired_amount` (`Lua/Buildings/StorageDepot.lua:68`,
   `Lua/Buildings/MultiResourceDepot.lua:221`, `Lua/Buildings/ResourceStockpile.lua:332`). A demand
   desired of 0 means the station wants filling to its physical capacity.
3. **The station's demand is a storage request, branded like a depot's.** `MultiResourceDepot.lua:33-34`
   (the station's base) and `StorageDepot.lua:353-354` (the Food Depot) carry the same
   `rfStorageDepot` flags, and every such request sits at priority 0
   (`CommonLua/TaskRequest.lua:183-189`). A depot-to-station haul is therefore vanilla's own
   depot-to-depot rebalance, decided inside the C matcher behind `Lua/_TaskRequest.lua:74-86`
   (`Request_FindTask`). No Lua decides it; `Request_FindSupply` / `Request_FindDemand` are the
   same C seam (`:56-72`).
4. **Vanilla has no supply-side floor.** The slider's own tooltip
   (`Lua/XDef/ipWasteRockSite.generated.lua:71`): "Drones and Shuttles will attempt to stockpile at
   least <DesiredAmountUI> ... The storages with higher Desired Amount will be filled first ... The
   stored resources will still be used when needed." Engine fact `EF-059` measured the same:
   `desired_amount` withholds nothing from the supply side. A station declared "as if desired is
   max" is the highest-desired storage on the map, so it outranks every depot and pulls them below
   their own floors. That is the owner's screenshot (`StorageFood` Desired 50 at 8.2/180 beside a
   `Food · Export` row at 16.5/120) at mechanism level.
5. **Nothing else hauls.** `30_TrainHubDrones.lua` makes no `FindTask` / `FindSupplyRequest` /
   `FindDemandRequest` call of its own (grep: none), so the vanilla matcher is the only actor.
6. **The row's slider never reaches the drone side today.** Vanilla's send branch ignores the
   amount, and spec §4.8's mode table says Export drones keep the station full; the slider is the
   trains' floor (`train_view`, `40_TrainDistribution.lua:599-612`). No slider behaviour changes.

### The change (narrowest; rung 1, same rung as today)

Stop the station from pulling. Export's drone baseline becomes **demand desired = max, supply
desired = max** instead of today's 0 and max. The station then asks for nothing on its own
initiative and gives nothing away; what reaches it is whatever vanilla pushes into a storage that
wants nothing.

- Where: `D.Apply`, after the existing `pcall(set_desired, st, n)` at
  `40_TrainDistribution.lua:316`, one extra write for `entry.mode == "export"`:
  `st.demand[res]:SetDesiredAmount(st:GetMaxStorageForAnyOneResource())`. That is the request
  writer `Station:SetDesiredAmount` itself calls (`Station.lua:978,984,989`), so the state stays
  vanilla-shaped. The header comment's "Export's drone baseline is full" line changes to say
  "Export's drone baseline pulls nothing".
- Residual without the mod: unchanged class. A player who touches the slider or the accept state
  rewrites both numbers (`Station.lua:1038-1039` store, `:1044-1045` export), and `vanilla_baseline`
  (`40_TrainDistribution.lua:288-292`) still restores the dial at save for hubless rows.
- No persisted name moves. No ban is crossed. Hub-served and hubless rows share `D.Apply`, so one
  change covers both; brief 35's battery carries the hub-served run.
- Stores with no Desired Amount of their own: a producer's output stockpile creates its supply
  request with `desired_amount` (default 0) and no storage brand
  (`ResourceStockpile.lua:328-332`), so `EF-059` says it wins every pairing before any depot and
  goes to any storage with room. **The call:** all of a producer's output is excess, exactly as
  vanilla feeds a depot. Record it in the commit with these lines.
- UI text to revisit in the same commit: the Export tooltips at `45_TrainDistributionUI.lua:41`
  and `:47` say "Local drones fill this station"; the wording depends on the measured outcome
  below.

## Fix 2 — no train ever appears in the hub; auto-fill is cut for vanilla's add-train

Revised under the owner's second ruling (2026-10-02, `cfc2065`, spec §4.8 ruling 10): *"If the
add train is something the player needs to do that is also fine. I don't want it to be a
complicated thing, and I don't want them spawned in the hub."* The first version of this plan
(commit `7870319`) assigned the auto-fill train at the joining station and kept the hub-side siding
handler for vanilla's assign-train path on a hub. Both parts are superseded below.

### The audit's reading: confirmed

- `70_TrainBay.lua:109` is `arm.track:AssignTrain(hub)`, inside `try_fill`, which picks a free
  hub arm (`:93-97`) and assigns on the hub. Confirmed.
- `:76-91` is `OnMsg.TransportLinkChanged`: when the vehicle's `current_station` is a hub and it is
  `at_spawn_track`, it moves the train to `Floor.HubSpawnLocation(hub, idx)`
  (`20_TrainHub.lua:402-404`, the computed siding of `synthetic_spot_pos(..., "Spawn", idx)`
  `:362-387`). Confirmed. Commit `9b58888` says it covers "extras, auto-fill and player spawns".
- `HubTrain` (`70_TrainBay.lua:6-9`) is a bare class with `persist_baseclass = "Train"`, persisted
  name #15 (`FIX_POLICY.md:179`). No other dev file, test or bug record references it (grep:
  none outside the bay file). It stays; nothing here touches it.

### How a train comes to exist, and where (vanilla)

`TrackBase:AssignTrain(station)` (`Lua/Buildings/Track.lua:428-457`) is the only spawn. It places
the Train **in the station passed**: `PlaceObjectIn("Train", station)`, position and angle from the
station's `Spawn<idx>` spot (`GetSpawnPoint`, `:206-209`), `current_station = station`,
`at_spawn_track = true`, then `AddTransportLink` (`Lua/Buildings/StationsLink.lua:36-47`),
`AssignToTrack`, `station:AddOccupyingTrain(train)` and `Start()`. It refuses silently when the
colony's train pool is empty, the route is at its cap or the spawn platform is taken (`:430-435`).

Vanilla gives the player two buttons on every station's card, and both reach that spawn with the
station as the argument (`Lua/XDef/customStation.generated.lua:11-40`):

- **Construct Train** calls `Station:ConstructTrain(1)` (`Lua/Buildings/Station.lua:707-745`): one
  train queued at the station's own metal and machine-parts cost. When it completes,
  `ConstructTrainInternal` (`:579-612`) adds one prefab to the pool and, if exactly one connected
  route is under its cap, assigns the train there at once; with two eligible routes it leaves the
  train in the pool for the second button.
- **Send out Train** (`ToggleCreateRouteMode`, `:796-803`, enabled only while the pool is above
  zero, `ToggleCreateRouteMode_Update` `:787-794`) lets the player click a connected track; the
  station then calls `track:AssignTrain(self)` (`InteractWithObject`, `:861-868`, eligibility in
  `CanInteractWithObject`, `:834-856`).

The command-centre Transportation overview offers the same `ConstructTrain(±1)` per station row
(`Lua/XDef/CommandCenterTransportationOverviewRow.generated.lua:477-483`).

A train born `at_spawn_track` leaves on its own: `LoadTrain` treats the spawn state as
`should_move` and may reverse onto its own track (`Lua/Units/Train.lua:237-258`), and the station's
`TrainDepart` clears the flag on the way out (`Station.lua:1209`).

### The hub shows both buttons today

The card's custom section is resolved by class name up the parent chain
(`Lua/XDef/sectionCustom.generated.lua:14-38`: `XDefs["custom" .. class]`, then the building
template's `object_class`), so a hub, which inherits `Station`, renders `customStation` with
**Construct Train** and **Send out Train**. The dev mod hides neither: `20_TrainHub.lua:244-249`
drops only the shared power-grid summary "to leave panel room for the train controls", and
`add_hub_sections` (`:3342-3375`) adds the drone and track-work sections beside them. So today a
player can queue a train at the hub or send one out from it, and either lands in the hub.

### Decision 1: cut auto-fill, keep vanilla's add-train at the station

Cutting is simpler, and it is the choice:

- **Less code, not more.** Assigning at the joining station needs a snapshot that keeps station
  sets instead of counts, a joining-station rule for multi-station joins, a track pick at that
  station and new test assertions. Cutting deletes `hub_lines`, `free_arm`, `try_fill`, the tick,
  the snapshot, the owed window and the siding handler (`70_TrainBay.lua:31-158`, about 110
  lines) and retires two test files.
- **Nothing new for the player to learn.** The two vanilla buttons above are the add-train the
  player already uses on every station; a constructed train assigns itself when the station has one
  route. There is no second mechanism to explain, which is the owner's "not a complicated thing".
- **No new behaviour to prove.** The smoke for a cut is "the hub refuses, the station works", both
  vanilla paths with vanilla's own witnesses.

What the cut leaves in the bay's file: the `HubTrain` class (persisted name, untouched) and the hub
refusal below. `B.Require` keeps `TrackBase.AssignTrain`, now for the guard. The three tests
(`tests/train_fill_fixture.py`, `train_fill_smoke.py`, `train_spawn_smoke.py`) and the cut bytes go
to a new folder under `docs/archive/` beside `train_bay_extras_20260928/`, append-only, with a
README on the same pattern (restore paths, build history `9947552`, `989bd56`, `9b58888`,
`378bd39`, this cut's commit). `Floor.HubSpawnLocation` (`20_TrainHub.lua:402-404`) has no other
caller once the handler and `train_spawn_smoke.py` are gone; it is removed in the same commit if
the hub file is otherwise untouched by the move, else reported.

### Decision 2: the hub refuses add-train, at the one spawn and on the card

Closing the path is not complicated. Everything sits on the hub's own class or in the bay file:

1. **The guard at the one spawn.** `TrackBase:AssignTrain(station)` is wrapped (it is already in
   `B.Require`): when `station` is a hub, print one `[TrainBay] refused hub=<handle>` line and
   return before vanilla's thread is created. This closes every caller at once: the two card
   buttons, the Transportation overview, the station-side auto-assign after construction, any
   console or mod call. A refusal costs nothing: vanilla's own early returns (`Track.lua:430-435`)
   already make `AssignTrain` a silent no-op when the pool or the platform is not ready, so callers
   expect nothing back.
2. **The buttons say why.** `InfopanelButton:OnContextUpdate` calls
   `context:<OnPressParam>_Update(button)` when the method exists
   (`Lua/XDef/InfopanelButton.generated.lua:59-64`). Four small methods on `SMROptInTrainHubBase`:
   `ConstructTrain_Update(button)` and `ToggleCreateRouteMode_Update(button)` disable each button
   with the rollover "Trains are added at a station, not at the hub"; `ConstructTrain()` and
   `ToggleCreateRouteMode()` return without acting, so the overview row and a gamepad press do
   nothing. The Trains section's count stays visible.
3. **Nothing else spawns at a hub.** `AssignTrain` is the only `PlaceObjectIn("Train", ...)`
   outside save loading (grep over `Lua/`: `Track.lua:437` only). `HubRestoreParkedTrains`
   (`20_TrainHub.lua:564-580`) re-places trains already parked or spawned at a hub in an old save;
   such a train leaves on its next `LoadTrain` and none is spawned there again. Trains passing
   through or stopping at the hub's sidings are movement, finished (owner, 2026-09-21), and not
   this fix.

Rungs: the guard and the four methods are rung 0 (nothing reaches the save). The cut removes a
runtime-only table (`owed` was never persisted). Save contract unchanged.

## Fix 3 — a one-unit food gain on unload at the hub

Added under `201fc95` (owner, 2026-10-02, routed from brief 33). The finding: log
`Mars.exe-20261002-15.30.19-6aba6e65.log` lines 534-537, one `UnloadAll` by `Train(2000002659)` at
hub 6430 on R1 at game time 35860002: `station_delta=13 train_delta=-12` for Food and
`station_delta=17 train_delta=-16` for Sugar, `size=1` each, whole units (the witness's `units()`
prints thousandths when there are any; there were none).

### What is confirmed on the page

- **The unload hands over the booked amount, and the train cannot go below zero.** Vanilla's
  `unload_cargo` (`Lua/Units/Train.lua:779-785`) does `station:AddResource(amount)` then
  `train:AddResource(-amount)` with the same `amount`, taken from `assigned_resources`
  (`UnloadAll`, `:787-831`, whole entries only). The train's `AddResource` is
  `MultiResourceCubeVisuals.AddResource` (`Lua/Buildings/MultiResourceCubeVisuals.lua:422-435`):
  "Floor at 0 but never clamp to max", so a train that carries 12 while 13 is booked loses 12 and
  the station gains 13. That is exactly the signature, for both resources.
- **The hub side added nothing of its own.** The hub's `OnAfterRequestUpdate`
  (`20_TrainHub.lua:1967-1970`) runs `Floor.Reconcile`, which moves holds on request targets and
  never actual stock (`10_TrainFloor.lua:134-181`). The hub has no `AddResource` override. At a
  hub, the mod's `UnloadAll` wrapper takes the plain path (`D.HubFor(hub)` is nil, so no answers
  or claims; `40_TrainDistribution.lua:475-518`).
- **The load leg was watched and clean.** The witness brackets both `LoadResourceForStation` and
  `UnloadAll` (`tests/80_AgentSlots_crossing.lua.txt:290-291`) and logged no load mismatch for
  this train, so the train received what the source gave. The shortfall arose between load and
  unload, outside any bracketed call.
- **Refuted: "spoilage in transit" as a vanilla mechanism.** Every spoilage caller in the archived
  source is listed here (presence side, 5 hits for `CalcResourceSpoilage|FoodDecay` outside
  `Spoilage.lua` itself): the daily pass over the `ResourceStockpile` label and map
  `ResourcePile`s (`Lua/Spoilage.lua:66-91`), depots (`StorageDepot.lua:126`, `:1329`),
  consumption piles (`HasConsumption.lua:132`), recipe pools (`RecipeProductionBuilding.lua:60`)
  and the constant (`__const.lua:1065`). A Train joins only the `Train` label (`Train.lua:113`),
  is not a `ResourceStockpile` (`ResourceStockpile.lua:74` is the only adder) and has no supply
  requests to spoil through. Trains never spoil, as `60_StationSpoilage.lua:7` already says.
- **Not ours by any path read.** `60_StationSpoilage.lua` replaces `Station:SpoilStoredResources`
  with a no-op and keeps vanilla's on the hub; neither touches a train. No mod file writes a
  train's `stockpiled_amount` or `assigned_resources` (grep over both dev mods: reads only; the
  hub's `AddResource` calls at `20_TrainHub.lua:2745-2764` move hub stock to construction sites).
  The depot's cabin transfer books and moves the same `k` (`10_ElevatorDepotDev.lua:1584-1596`)
  and its witness control read `cabin_mismatch=0`.

### What is not found

Why the train carried one unit less than booked, for Food and Sugar at once, is not located by
reading. The remaining writers of a train's cargo table in vanilla are `SetStoredAmount`
(`MultiResourceCubeVisuals.lua:549-556`, called only from `Train:Done`), `ClearAllResources`
and `ReturnStockpiledResources`; none is food-specific. Two shapes fit the data and only a
measurement separates them:

1. An unbracketed writer lowered the train's `stockpiled_amount` for the two food resources by
   one unit each (something food-aware, which in this source means spoilage-shaped code, yet none
   reaches a train).
2. A booking that overstates: two loads for one destination (`Train.lua:757-767`) where the
   earlier load was taken at a station the witness kept no books for (`train_wrap` returns early
   without books, `80_AgentSlots_crossing.lua.txt:176-179`), so one leg went unwatched.

### Plan: measure first, then fix at the source or at our seam

The owner's outcome is "an unload adds to the station exactly what left the train, or a report
says why not". The cheapest step that can say which is a trap in the one smoke:

- **Train cargo trap** (a sitting-only slot, log-only): for every train, replace
  `stockpiled_amount` with a proxy whose `__newindex` logs the train, resource, old and new value,
  game time and a short traceback for any write that happens outside a `LoadResourceForStation` or
  `UnloadAll` bracket, and whether the train is at a station with books. Disarmed by an
  autosave; re-press it. Positive control: the first bracketed write it sees is logged once as
  `trap=armed`.
- Run it beside the crossing witness for one sol at top speed. Prediction: either the trap names
  the writer and its traceback (shape 1), or it stays silent while a `crossing_mismatch` recurs and
  the mismatch's train shows a load at a bookless station in the same log (shape 2). A silent trap
  with no recurrence is a no-result, not a pass; the sitting notes it and brief 35 carries it.

Then, in phase 2 or a follow-up the orchestrator briefs:

- **If the writer is ours**: fix it where it writes. Simple by construction, since no mod file
  writes train cargo today.
- **If the writer is vanilla or C-side, or shape 2 holds**: the fix that honours the outcome sits
  at the seam the mod already wraps, `Train:UnloadAll` (`40_TrainDistribution.lua:475`): before
  vanilla's unload, lower each destination's booked entry so the bookings of a resource never
  exceed what the train carries, unassigning the difference on that destination's demand
